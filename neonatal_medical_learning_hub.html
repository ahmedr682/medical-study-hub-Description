1. Scalp & Cranial Vault Birth Trauma

Mechanical forces during delivery (pressure against maternal pelvic bones or instrument assistance with forceps/vacuum) lead to distinct scalp and bone injuries. Differentiation relies on anatomical layers and suture lines.

Caput Succedaneum (Diffuse Subcutaneous Scalp Edema)Click to Expand ▼
Anatomical Layer Soft tissue edema above the epicranial aponeurosis.
Key Diagnostic Feature CROSSES suture lines.
Onset & Course: Present at birth; resolves spontaneously within 2–3 days; may lead to scalp molding for several weeks.
Cephalohematoma (Subperiosteal Hemorrhage)Click to Expand ▼
Anatomical Layer Subperiosteal hemorrhage bound by skull periosteum.
Key Diagnostic Feature Does NOT cross suture lines.
Onset & Course: Appears 12–24 hours post-delivery; resolves over 2 weeks to 3 months; may calcify or lead to jaundice (secondary to RBC breakdown); may harbor an underlying linear skull fracture.
Nerve Roots & Reflexes

2. Brachial Plexus Injury Spectrum

Erb-Duchenne Palsy (Upper Trunk: C5–C6)

Etiology: Excessive lateral traction on the neck during delivery (e.g., shoulder dystocia).
Presentation: Inability to abduct shoulder, externally rotate, or supinate forearm → "Waiter's Tip" posture (adducted, internally rotated arm, extended elbow, flexed wrist).
Reflex: Asymmetric or absent Moro reflex on the affected side.

Klumpke Palsy (Lower Trunk: C7–C8 ± T1)

Etiology: Excessive upward traction on the arm during delivery (e.g., breech delivery).
Presentation: Paralysis of intrinsic hand muscles resulting in a "Total Claw Hand".
Reflex & Autonomic: Absent grasp reflex; may co-exist with Horner Syndrome (triad of ptosis, miosis, and anhidrosis) due to T1 sympathetic chain injury.

Management Protocol: Proper positioning, partial immobilization, physiotherapy (massage and passive ROM exercises). Perform surgical neuroplasty if no recovery occurs within 3–6 months.
Clinical Correlations

3. Facial Nerve & Bone Trauma

Clavicular Fracture (Most Common Delivery Bone Injury)▼
Etiology: Shoulder dystocia in vertex deliveries or arm extension in breech presentations.
Signs: Asymmetric Moro reflex, local tenderness, and a palpable callus within one week.
Management: Immobilization of the arm and shoulder.
Facial Nerve Palsy (CN VII Compression)▼
Etiology: In utero pressure against the sacral promontory or forceps compression over the facial nerve.
Presentation: Unilateral weakness involving the entire side of the face including the forehead.
Management: Eye care (protective lubrication); neuroplasty if no improvement (indicating torn nerve fibers).
Type: Linear Skull Fracture → Cause: Pressure in utero or forceps. Outcome: Most common type; asymptomatic, no treatment required.

Systemic Examination Pearls

Head, Eye, & Extremity Exam Findings

Preauricular Tags / Pits

Infants with preauricular tags or pits must be evaluated for hearing loss and undergo renal ultrasound to screen for genitourinary anomalies.

Ocular Malformations

• Coloboma of Iris: Cleft at the 6 o'clock position; associated with CHARGE association.
• Aniridia: Hypoplasia of the iris (may extend to retina); associated with Wilms tumor.

Polydactyly: Presence of >5 fingers or toes. Requires no treatment if the extra digit has good blood supply and intact structural alignment.
IUGR Classification

Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR)

IUGR represents pathologically impaired fetal growth resulting in birth weight below expectations. The pattern of growth restriction identifies the timing and mechanism of fetal insult.

Head-Sparing (~70%)

Asymmetrical IUGR

• Timing & Cause: Relatively late onset after organogenesis; caused by abnormal nutrient/O2 delivery.
• Etiologies: Uteroplacental insufficiency secondary to maternal disease (hypertension, cardiac, renal, severe anemia) or placental abruption.
• Proportions: Head circumference is normal; body and limbs are thin and small.
• Complication: Neurologic hypoxia/asphyxia if oxygen delivery to brain drops.

Global Insult (~30%)

Symmetrical IUGR

• Timing & Cause: Early in utero insult affecting global organ growth and cell division.
• Etiologies: Genetic syndromes, chromosomal aneuploidy, congenital heart disease, teratogens, or early intrauterine TORCH infections.
• Proportions: Entire body is proportionally small; head circumference is proportional to body size.

Complications of IUGR: Stillbirth, preterm labor, low birth weight (<2500 g) with ↑ risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS), perinatal asphyxia, and potential long-term motor/neurological disabilities.
