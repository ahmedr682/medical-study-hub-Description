Core definition

Family medicine is the medical specialty that provides continuing and comprehensive health care for the individual and the family.

Its scope encompasses all ages, both genders, each organ system and every disease entity.

Definition of the family physician

Family physicians provide continuing and comprehensive medical care, health maintenance and preventive services to each family member regardless of gender, age or type of problem — biological, behavioural or social.

Remember: Family medicine is not limited to one organ, age group, gender or disease category. It combines ongoing care with prevention and a broad view of the person.

Primary care

Primary care is provided by physicians specifically trained and skilled in comprehensive first-contact and continuing care for ill people or those with an undiagnosed sign, symptom or health concern — the “undifferentiated” patient. It is not limited by the problem’s biological, behavioural or social origin, organ system or gender.

Six functions of primary care

  1. First-contact care: entry point into the healthcare system.
  2. Continuity: care during sickness and health over time.
  3. Comprehensiveness: draws on all traditional major disciplines.
  4. Coordination: coordinates all healthcare needs.
  5. Continuing responsibility: individual follow-up and community health problems.
  6. Personalization: highly personalized care.

Characteristics and functions of the family physician

  1. Continuing responsibility
  2. Comprehensive care
  3. Interpersonal skills
  4. Accessibility
  5. Diagnostic skills for undifferentiated problems
  6. Coordinator of care

1 · Continuing responsibility

The commitment does not end when the illness ends. The longitudinal relationship is characterized by trust, loyalty and responsibility, and is especially valuable in chronic diseases such as asthma and diabetes.

The lecture notes that continuing comprehensive care is associated with fewer hospitalizations, operations and illness-related physician visits. Knowing the patient may allow earlier assessment of acute problems and prevention of complications.

2 · Comprehensive care

Consider factors that may help or hinder recovery: religious beliefs, social, economic and cultural problems, personal expectations and heredity. Spiritual, intellectual, emotional, social and economic factors can affect illness.

Early identification of disease in its undifferentiated stage requires specific training.

3 · Interpersonal skills

Use knowledge of interpersonal relations effectively in patient management.

4 · Accessibility

The patient’s sense of security from knowing the physician can be reached in person or by phone can have a therapeutic, comforting and calming influence.

5 · Diagnostic skills

Patients often present early, before a diagnosis is clear. Symptoms may be vague and nonspecific, and signs minimal or absent. The family physician must be skilled at assessing undifferentiated problems.

6 · Coordinator

As medicine becomes more specialized and complex, the family physician integrates health services, facilitates access to the wider system and interprets the system’s activities to the patient.

Doctor–patient relationship: patient-centred care

Effective communication helps the physician support the patient. A key early step is establishing rapport.

Five communication practices

  1. Minimize distractions and interruptions: silence your phone; reduce outside noise and ask permission before closing the door.
  2. Use active listening: attend to verbal and non-verbal communication; paraphrase and restate; consider both facts and emotions.
  3. Use deliberate non-verbal cues: lean forward, maintain eye contact, nod appropriately and avoid crossing your arms.
  4. Offer concrete feedback: summarize with “Let me see if I have this right…” and explore feelings with open prompts.
  5. Allow correction: ask “Did I miss anything?” and let the patient add or correct details.
The lecture highlights that empathy can improve diagnostic accuracy, therapeutic adherence and patient satisfaction.

How to interact with special patients

1 · Anxious patient

  • Encourage the patient to talk about feelings.
  • Ask what is causing the anxiety.
  • Offer reasonable reassurance.
  • Validate the response: acknowledge that someone in the same situation might feel similarly, and explain that you will do your best to address concerns.

2 · Angry patient

  • Stay calm and do not become frightened.
  • Allow the patient to express feelings.
  • Ask about the reason for the anger.
  • Address the concern reasonably and validate the patient’s feelings. If the issue is a long wait, acknowledge the delay and apologize rather than arguing.

3 · Patient in pain

  • Show compassion and acknowledge the pain.
  • Ask whether anything can make the patient more comfortable.
  • Do not repeat painful examination maneuvers.
  • If examination is necessary, explain why, reassure the patient that you will be quick and gentle, and seek cooperation.
Clinical communication principle: acknowledge the emotion or discomfort first, explore the concern, explain the next step and preserve the patient’s dignity.

Counseling in primary care

Counselling about lifestyle modification and psychosocial problems is a fundamental family physician competency. The lecture notes that chronic illness visits commonly involve psychosocial factors, and that health-risk behaviour counselling and health education are part of primary care.

Even when a behaviour is not directly related to the presenting diagnosis, address it briefly and with care. Link advice to objective facts where possible. Patients may respond better to “I recommend…” than “You should…”.

The Five A’s

1 · Ask

Ask about the behaviour, e.g. tobacco use: “Do you smoke cigarettes?”

2 · Advise

Give clear, personalized advice to stop: “I strongly recommend that you quit smoking.”

3 · Assess

Assess willingness, importance and self-efficacy. Ask whether the patient wants to become tobacco-free and whether they believe they can quit successfully.

4 · Assist

Help develop a quit plan and recommend approved medication if needed.

5 · Arrange

Arrange follow-up in person or by telephone; refer for specialist support if needed.
